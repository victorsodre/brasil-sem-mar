# brasil-sem-mar

Not about landlocked countries. It is Brazil with the sea removed: the real relief of each place, above and below the water, in neon 3D in the browser.

`npx brasil-sem-mar <place>` downloads open data (Copernicus DEM on land, GMRT at sea), writes one terrain per place, and the Three.js page draws the relief as neon lines on black and "drains" the water. One command, no API key, one stack (Node 22+, no Python, no build step). The headless capture exports deterministic 1080x1920 PNG sequences and MP4 for Shorts and for X.

The thesis is "Brazil with a name, a number and a source": every place carries `NUMEROS.md` (each number with a primary source and URL), `PROVA-INEDITA.md` (what already exists on video about it) and `DIARIO.md` (what was built and what broke, dated). The UI and the notes are in Brazilian Portuguese; identifiers are in English.

## Use it in 30 seconds

```sh
git clone https://github.com/victorsodre/brasil-sem-mar && cd brasil-sem-mar
npm install
npx brasil-sem-mar 002-vitoria-trindade   # downloads Copernicus + GMRT, writes lugares/002-vitoria-trindade/terreno.png
npm run serve                              # http://127.0.0.1:8765/
```

Open `http://127.0.0.1:8765/index.html?lugar=002-vitoria-trindade`. "Reproduzir" plays the camera track, "Tempo" jumps to any second, the "Esvaziar" slider lowers the water level, the mouse (or a finger) orbits. Deep link by time: `&t=12` (the UI opens in sync with the scene). `npx brasil-sem-mar` is the package `bin` (it also works through the symlink npm creates); `node cli/index.mjs <place>` does the same. Places live in `./lugares/<slug>/`, in the current folder.

Capture (needs Playwright's Chromium: `npx playwright install chromium`; MP4 needs `ffmpeg` in PATH):

```sh
node captura/quadro.mjs 002-vitoria-trindade --t 12             # one frame (t = 12 s), quick preview
node captura/capture.mjs 002-vitoria-trindade --de 0 --ate 10   # PNG sequence + MP4, 30 fps, 1080x1920
```

Output in `captura/saida/<slug>/`. The capture is deterministic: the same `t` gives the same frame, byte for byte (the report records the diff between two runs of 3 frames; it must be 0). Pure black background, no text: the video pipeline composites the sequence as a light layer.

## Create a new place

1. Create `lugares/<slug>/lugar.json` (template: `lugares/002-vitoria-trindade/lugar.json`; all fields in `SPEC.md`): name, `bbox` (north, south, west, east, degrees), `exagero_vertical`, `terra` (`copernicus` or `gmrt`), `mar` (`gmrt`), `mascara`, `regua` and the `frase_de_vida` with its source.
2. Or pass the box directly and the CLI writes the `lugar.json` with the defaults: `npx brasil-sem-mar --bbox <north>,<south>,<west>,<east> --slug 003-my-place --nome "My place"`. Options: `npx brasil-sem-mar --ajuda`.
3. `npx brasil-sem-mar 003-my-place` downloads (cached in `lugares/<slug>/cache/`, gitignored), merges land and sea, and writes `terreno.png` (Terrain-RGB) and `terreno.json` (provenance: exact URL of every call, bytes, sha256, licence, measured vs interpolated fraction).
4. Write `camera.json` (61 s keyframe track): `node scripts/picos.mjs <slug>` lists the seamounts in scene coordinates for the keyframes and `node scripts/trilha-check.mjs <slug>` checks the track against the terrain (clearance, speed, horizon, where the ruler lands on screen) without a browser. Then `NUMEROS.md`, `PROVA-INEDITA.md` and `DIARIO.md`. The `lugar.json` also takes `regua.escala` (visible factor of the ruler over the exaggeration) and an optional `nevoa_por_km` (fog per km; default 0.0022).
5. Budget per place: 10 MB on GitHub Pages, texture up to 2048, up to 1 million triangles, 30 fps on a phone.

## What already exists

"Draining the sea" is not new (URLs checked on 2026-10-13): NASA SVS "Draining the Oceans" (2008, 2020, and James O'Donoghue's 2019 4K remake), National Geographic's "Drain the Oceans" (since 2018, 6 seasons, 63 episodes), Google Earth 5.0 (2009, GEBCO_08 seafloor), earth-drained (snapidapp, 2026-10-06, ETOPO at ~18 km per node), Seascape (Open Waters, open bathymetry tiles; for Brazil it records "closed / request-only, no open hi-res source exists"), OpenZenith DEM (Copernicus GLO-30 + GEBCO 2025 tiles) and bopen/elevation (`eio clip`). Links in the Portuguese README.

The difference is not "nobody did 3D of the seafloor". It is Brazil with a name, a number and a source: the Brazilian margin with the features the LEPLAC map gathers (and, in part, named): Besnard Bank, Vema Channel, Santa Catarina Plateau, Rio Grande Rise, each one a forkable `lugar.json` with primary-sourced numbers, a recorded originality check, and a camera at sea level, never a map view.

## Honesty on screen

- Vertical exaggeration is declared per place (001: 30x; 002: 15x) and said in the voice-over and in every description. The 30 m Christ the Redeemer ruler follows the relief exaggeration times `regua.escala` declared in `lugar.json`: in 002 it is 1.5 (30 m x 15 x 1.5 = 675 m on screen, standing on a 122 m hill at the mouth of Vitória bay), and the page and the voice-over say so. Never an ambiguous scale.
- Measured vs interpolated: GMRT's `topo-mask` layer says, node by node, what is multibeam (value) and what is interpolated from the GEBCO base (NaN). The fraction goes into `terreno.json` and the description. In the whole 002 box, 8.4 % of the sea nodes are measured (66,900 of 792,363); the Besnard Bank box came out 0 %: the summit shape in the render is GEBCO's; the 30 m depth is the Navy's (LEPLAC, via Pesquisa FAPESP).
- The real resolution of each source goes into `terreno.json` and the description: in 002 the Copernicus land came in at overview 3 (about 247 m per node) and the GMRT sea at 458 m, both resampled to 636 m per node; the 30 m only show up in a small box. 001 is GMRT only, at about 1.9 km per node.
- The 40 m grid of the ridge (CORE-VTRCC) is not in the repository yet, and even when it arrives it does not cover summits shallower than 100 to 200 m (the paper says so).
- No invented riverbeds, reservoir floors or coastlines. What breaks is listed in `ONDE-QUEBRA.md`.

## Altered or synthetic content label

By YouTube's three criteria (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491) the label is "no": no real person is made to say or do anything, no footage of a real event or place is altered, and neon relief computed from a DEM is not a realistic scene of something that did not happen. Cloning one's own voice for voice-overs is on YouTube's list of what does not need disclosure.

## For anyone touching the code

`AGENTS.md` lists the traps; `SPEC.md` is the contract between modules; `ONDE-QUEBRA.md` lists what does not look right and why. Tests: `npm test`. Check a terrain against the cached GMRT GeoTIFF at 20 fixed points: `node cli/conferir.mjs <slug>`. Check a camera track against the terrain without a browser: `node scripts/trilha-check.mjs <slug>`.
